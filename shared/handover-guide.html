Six steps to your own address

  1. Buy your web address You · 5 min

    Go to domains.squarespace.com, search thecicadacloset.com and buy it. It matches your Instagram handle, so people type what they already know.

    • You only need the domain. Skip any website plan or builder Squarespace offers; your site is already built.
    • Use an email you check, and leave auto-renew on. If the address ever lapses, the site goes dark until it's renewed.

    Taking the Update Plan? Skip this step. We buy the address for you and pay the renewals.

  2. Make a free GitHub account You · 5 min

    GitHub is where your website's files will live, and it hosts the site for free. Go to github.com, click Sign up and choose the free plan.

    • Turn on two-factor sign-in when it offers. It keeps anyone else out of your site.
    • Send us your username, the name shown on your profile, not your email.

  3. Accept your website You · 1 min

    We send the site to your account. GitHub emails you an invitation to take it over; click Accept.

    That invitation expires after one day, so tell us when you're ready and we'll send it then.

  4. Connect your address You · 15 min

    Four short parts, in this order. Stuck anywhere? Send us a screenshot and we'll tell you exactly what to click.

    1. Claim the address in GitHub. Click your profile picture (top right) → Settings → Pages (left side, under “Code, planning, and automation”) → Add a domain. Type thecicadacloset.com and click Add domain. GitHub shows you a TXT record; leave that page open.
    2. Point your website at it. Open your website on GitHub (it's listed on your profile) → Settings → Pages. Under Custom domain, type thecicadacloset.com and click Save. A DNS warning at this point is normal.
    3. Add the records in Squarespace. Go to Domains → thecicadacloset.com → DNS. Delete the Squarespace Defaults records (they point the address at Squarespace), then add the custom records listed below.
    AHost@Data185.199.108.153
    AHost@Data185.199.109.153
    AHost@Data185.199.110.153
    AHost@Data185.199.111.153
    CNAMEHostwwwDatayour-username.github.io
    TXTHost_github-pages-challenge-your-usernameDatathe code GitHub showed you
    For the TXT record, Squarespace wants only the part of the name before .thecicadacloset.com.
    1. Finish the claim. Back in GitHub's Settings → Pages, open the ··· menu beside your domain → Continue verifying → Verify. If it isn't found yet, try again in an hour.

    On the Update Plan we do part 3 for you. Just send us a screenshot of the TXT record from part 1.

    The four A records are GitHub's own addresses. The TXT record proves the address is yours, so no one else can ever point it anywhere.

  5. Go live Us · within 48 hours

    Addresses usually connect within minutes, but can take up to 48 hours. Once it's through:

    • We message you to tick Enforce HTTPS in Settings → Pages. That's the padlock in the browser bar, and it's free.
    • We turn on Google visibility, check the site on phones and computers, and retire the preview link.
    • We send you a complete backup copy of the site.

  6. Tell people where to find you You · 5 min

    Put thecicadacloset.com in your Instagram bio and in the website field of your Google Business Profile. Both help people, and Google, find the new site.

Questions you might have

Do I really own it?

Yes. The website's files sit in your own GitHub account. If you buy the web address, it's registered in your name; on the Update Plan we hold it for you and hand it over whenever you ask. Nothing depends on us to keep running.

Is there any monthly fee?

No. Hosting on GitHub is free. The only cost is your web address, about $20 a year to Squarespace, which renews automatically. The Update Plan below is optional, and it includes your web address.

Can I change things myself?

The files are yours, so technically yes. But the site is hand-built code, and one misplaced character can break a section, so we recommend sending changes to us. If anything ever does break, GitHub keeps every past version, so it can always be put back.

Why is my site's code visible on GitHub?

Free hosting on GitHub needs the files to be public. They're the same files anyone already sees by visiting your website. There are no passwords, customer details or anything private in them.

What happens if I don't renew the address?

The site stops showing at thecicadacloset.com until it's renewed, and someone else could buy the address. Your website files stay safe in GitHub either way. Keep auto-renew on. On the Update Plan, we renew it for you.

Can I get an email address at thecicadacloset.com?

Yes, though it isn't needed for the website. Squarespace sells professional email through Google Workspace for a monthly fee; you can add it from your Squarespace domain settings whenever you like.

How soon will I show up on Google?

We switch Google visibility on the day you go live. New sites usually take days to a few weeks to appear in results. Linking it from your Google Business Profile helps.

What if you're not around one day?

The site keeps running on its own. It's standard web files, so any web developer can work on it, and you'll have your own backup copy.
