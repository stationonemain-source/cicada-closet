The studio

A small studio with green walls, a keyhole in its logo, and an owner who is a fine artist as well as a tattooer.

The Cicada Closet opened in 2026 on South Jenkins Avenue, a few blocks from campus. It is owned and run by Jenna Feezel, a licensed tattoo artist with a Bachelor of Fine Arts, whose work leans toward moths, birds, beetles and botanicals, in black and grey and in colour.

The walls double as a small gallery. Local artists can hang work there and keep 100% of what sells, with no commission and no fees.

The artist

Jenna Feezel

Jenna holds a Bachelor of Fine Arts and paints as well as tattoos. Her BFA exhibition and her paintings sit alongside the tattoo work on her Instagram.

Her subjects come from the field and the garden: luna moths, bluebirds, stag beetles, salamanders, morning glories and, fittingly, cicadas.

Aftercare

The first weeks matter most.

Follow the aftercare instructions Jenna gives you. Her Aftercare highlight on Instagram has her version. These are the general basics most artists agree on.

The first night

Leave the wrap on as long as Jenna told you. When it comes off, wash gently with lukewarm water and unscented soap, then pat dry with a clean paper towel.

Days two to fourteen

Wash twice a day. Apply a thin layer of the ointment or lotion she recommends. Thin means you can barely see it.

Leave it alone

No picking, no scratching, no soaking. Showers are fine. Baths, pools, lakes and hot tubs are not until it is fully healed.

Sun

Keep it out of direct sun while healing. After that, sunscreen is what keeps fine line fine.
